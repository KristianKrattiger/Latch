EXAMPLE 1

Context:
[p0] Heaven Help Us was released on October 5 1985.
[p1] Ghar Ki Rani was released on 1991-03-12.

Question: Which film was released first, Heaven Help Us or Ghar Ki Rani?
Expected answer type: entity

Output:
[
  {"step_id": "s1", "operator": "temporal_order",
   "args": [
     {"ref_type": "span", "doc_id": "p0", "text": "October 5 1985"},
     {"ref_type": "span", "doc_id": "p1", "text": "1991-03-12"}
   ],
   "params": {"op": "before"}, "result_type": "bool", "is_final": true}
]

(temporal_order IS the final step here — it directly answers "which came first" with
a bool. Do NOT add a conjoin step afterwards. conjoin takes only bool results from
prior steps (e.g. conjoin(s1, s2) where s1 and s2 are already bools); never pass
date spans or entity spans directly to conjoin. For date comparison: use
temporal_order. For entity comparison: use entity_equal. Those are always is_final.)

EXAMPLE 2

Context:
[p0] Empress Xu's husband was the Yongle Emperor.
[p1] The Yongle Emperor was born in Nanjing.
[p2] Emperor Hongxi was born in Nanjing.

Question: Were Empress Xu's husband and Emperor Hongxi born in the same city?
Expected answer type: bool

Output:
[
  {"step_id": "s1", "operator": "entity_equal",
   "args": [
     {"ref_type": "span", "doc_id": "p1", "text": "Nanjing"},
     {"ref_type": "span", "doc_id": "p2", "text": "Nanjing"}
   ],
   "result_type": "bool", "is_final": true}
]

(This question has two retrieval hops — find husband, find birthplace — then one
operator step: entity_equal on the two birthplaces. entity_equal IS the final step;
do not add conjoin after it. Use steps whenever the question ends in a comparison,
count, or arithmetic operation, even if the retrieval leading up to it is implicit.
Only emit [] when the question is PURE retrieval with no operator at the end — e.g.
"What city was X born in?" has no operator and needs no steps. "Were X and Y born in
the same city?" ends in entity_equal and needs a step.

entity_equal compares the TEXT of two spans verbatim. For nationality questions
("same nationality?"), the args must be the NATIONALITY strings, not the person's
name. Find the nationality word in the director's bio doc — e.g. "French" from
"was a French film director" — and use that as the span text. Never pass a person's
name to entity_equal for a nationality question.)

EXAMPLE 5

Context:
[p0] Film A is a 1945 Argentine film directed by Carlos Borcosque.
[p1] Carlos Borcosque (9 September 1894 – 5 September 1965) was a Chilean film director.
[p2] Film B is a 1968 Italian film directed by Maurice Cloche.
[p3] Maurice Cloche (17 June 1907 – 23 March 1990) was a French film director.

Question: Which film has the director who died earlier, Film A or Film B?
Expected answer type: entity

Output:
[
  {"step_id": "s1", "operator": "temporal_order",
   "args": [
     {"ref_type": "span", "doc_id": "p1", "text": "5 September 1965"},
     {"ref_type": "span", "doc_id": "p3", "text": "23 March 1990"}
   ],
   "params": {"op": "before"}, "result_type": "bool", "is_final": true}
]

(bridge_comparison rule: use the director's OWN biography document for dates, NOT
the film document that merely mentions them. Here p0 and p2 are film docs — ignore
their production years as proxies for director dates. p1 and p3 are director bio
docs, identifiable by the pattern "Name (DATE – DATE) was a director".

For "died earlier/later": extract the DEATH date — the second date in the range,
after the dash. "5 September 1965" is a verbatim substring of p1; it passes
provenance and is unambiguous. Do NOT submit the full range "9 September 1894 –
5 September 1965" for a death-date question — the gate will parse the first date
(birth) instead.

For "born earlier/later": extract the BIRTH date — the first date before the dash.
The full range also works for birth questions since the gate takes the first date.

Summary: film doc → film release dates only. Director bio doc → birth/death dates.
Never use a film's production year as a director's birth or death date.)
